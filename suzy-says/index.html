A familiar room, an unfamiliar set of rules

Suzy Says is a first-person rules-based survival horror game built around a deceptively familiar situation: arriving home and discovering an interactive DVD alongside colorful floor mats. Those everyday objects provide the setting for instructions inspired by Simon Says. Instead of treating every command as an invitation to move immediately, you need to understand the condition attached to it. The tension comes from having to make that distinction while a horror presentation competes for your attention. This guide introduces that premise without pretending to provide a verified solution to every command or a guaranteed path through the hosted game.

For visitors arriving from Survive Min, the important difference is how you participate. Here the known controls involve movement, jumping and crouching rather than only selecting dialogue. Reading carefully still matters, but it has to be translated into a physical action inside a first-person space. Approach the first session as an opportunity to understand the relationship between instruction and response. You do not need advance knowledge of a hidden ending to begin, and there is no reason to treat a surprising early result as proof that you have permanently ruined your run.

Before pressing Play

The browser adaptation uses Construct 3 and has an approximately 256 MB initial load. That is substantial for a game embedded in a page, especially on a metered connection or an older device. Read the warnings and controls before starting the download. The cover is only the launch surface; seeing it does not mean the game resources have already arrived. After selecting Play, allow the embedded game time to initialize rather than pressing the launch control repeatedly or refreshing at the first blank moment. No exact loading time is promised because connection speed, device resources and caching vary.

This is horror content with flashing imagery, jump scares and unsettling themes. Decide whether that is appropriate for you before entering the player. Reducing volume may make sudden sounds less intrusive, but it does not remove flashing visuals or disturbing material. If these elements are a concern, skipping the game is a reasonable choice. The videos further down this page can also contain startling scenes and spoilers, so they are not a warning-free alternative. Keep a comfortable way to leave the session available and take a break whenever the experience stops being enjoyable.

Learn the controls without inventing extra commands

The supplied keyboard controls are WASD for movement, Space for jumping and C for crouching. These are the controls to prepare before attempting an instruction under pressure. Position your hand so that reaching Space or C does not make you lose track of the movement keys. A first-person view can make orientation feel unfamiliar, so use whatever safe introductory opportunity the game actually offers to understand your position. Do not assume a separate sprint key, a particular interaction button or a pause shortcut from another game; follow the information shown by this version instead.

If a key appears to affect the surrounding webpage rather than the game, click inside the embedded player and try again when the game is ready. Keyboard focus and loading are different problems, and neither necessarily means the command itself was wrong. Avoid testing several keys at once: an overlapping movement and jump makes it harder to understand which input produced the result. This recommendation is a learning method, not a claim about a required secret technique. Once you know that input reaches the player, return to the current instruction rather than repeating an older command from memory.

Separate the condition from the action

Simon Says-style instructions ask you to distinguish a valid command from an action that only sounds tempting. In Suzy Says, the useful habit is to identify the qualifying language before committing to movement. Read or listen to the complete instruction as it is presented, then decide what it asks and whether its condition applies. A familiar action word is not enough by itself. This page does not supply a universal phrase list or a timing window because those details have not been verified for the hosted adaptation. The instruction currently shown in the game takes priority over general advice.

Try describing the decision in two parts: the rule that makes the instruction actionable, and the movement needed to carry it out. Keeping those ideas separate can reduce impulsive reactions when the presentation becomes distracting. It also helps you review a failed attempt without blaming everything on reflexes. Perhaps you understood the action but missed the condition, or perhaps you understood the condition but pressed the wrong control. Those are different errors with different remedies. Neither requires assuming that the game changed its rules secretly, and neither establishes an unseen ending requirement.

Use the colored mats as observed information

The colorful floor mats are part of the premise, but this guide does not assign them invented safe colors, fixed sequences or hidden numerical values. Pay attention to what the current scene and instruction actually connect to them. When you are learning a space, noticing where a relevant object sits relative to your current viewpoint is more useful than memorizing a direction without context. A leftward movement from one position may not mean the same thing after you have turned or moved. Treat your present view as the reference rather than assuming a remembered route always applies.

If you want to keep notes, make them descriptive and small. Record the instruction you encountered, the action you attempted and the result you observed. Label uncertainty explicitly instead of turning a guess into a rule. For example, remembering that you were moving during a command is not the same as proving movement caused the outcome. This careful approach protects you from building an elaborate strategy on one ambiguous moment. It also makes a later comparison with footage more useful, because you can identify exactly which part of your observation remains unclear.

Make a second attempt informative

A repeat attempt is most useful when it answers a question. If you believe you misread a condition, focus on reading that condition next time. If you believe your input did not register, first check focus and controls. Changing your interpretation, route and input timing simultaneously may produce a different result, but it will not tell you which change mattered. Where the game allows a comparable attempt, adjust one thing and observe. This is practical experimentation rather than a walkthrough, and it cannot promise that every situation repeats identically or that progress is preserved between sessions.

Do not use a browser refresh as a casual undo action. Refreshing unloads the embedded game and may lose progress or trigger another resource load. Use the game's own menus or restart options when they are available and understandable, without assuming that an unverified save feature exists. The checklist on this page is deliberately separate: checking a preparation step does not communicate with the game or unlock anything. Its Reset button only clears your preparation marks. This distinction matters when you return later and want to know which information represents real in-game progress.

Browser loading and device expectations

A slow first load is plausible given the size of the adaptation, but a loading indicator alone cannot prove that everything is working. Allow time, check whether your connection is stable and avoid running unnecessary heavy tabs alongside the game. If the player never becomes usable, note the device, browser and visible symptom before trying again. A blank area, a stalled resource screen and keys not responding are different observations. There is no guaranteed fix listed here because we have not reproduced every failure mode. The page intentionally does not offer a separate top-level game link as a fallback.

Touch adaptation exists in the hosted version, but real-phone play has not been verified for this guide. A responsive outer page does not prove that every in-game control is comfortable on a small touchscreen, nor does it guarantee enough memory for a large first load. Keyboard play is the clearest documented control path. On a phone, consider connection cost, available screen space and whether the actual controls are readable before investing time in a run. Rotating a device can change available space, but it is not evidence of complete mobile compatibility or reliable completion.

If you are investigating a control problem, look for the relevant interaction rather than skipping straight to a final scene. A video can demonstrate what happened in a recorded session, but it cannot establish your current loading state, device support or whether a different version has exactly the same content.

If a feature is not visible in the version you load, do not assume that following an unsupported guide will make it appear.

Frequently asked questions

What is Suzy Says?

A first-person rules-based survival horror game involving an interactive DVD, colorful mats and Simon Says-style commands.

What are the keyboard controls?

Use WASD to move, Space to jump and C to crouch. Follow the current game's instructions for any other actions.

Why can the first load take a while?

The Construct 3 browser adaptation loads approximately 256 MB. Connection speed and device resources affect waiting time; no fixed duration is guaranteed.

Is phone play verified?

No. Touch adaptation exists, but real-phone play and completion have not been verified. Responsive page layout is not a guarantee of mobile game compatibility.

Does this version include endless play and the hidden ending?

Those features are described for the full release, but their presence in this hosted adaptation is unconfirmed. We do not promise a complete edition.

Are there content warnings?

Yes: flashing imagery, jump scares and unsettling themes. Avoid the game and optional footage if those elements are unsuitable for you.

Does the checklist save my game?

No. It is a local preparation aid. Reset clears its marks, and reloading the page also clears them; neither action creates an in-game save.

Can I read this guide without loading the game?

Yes. The game iframe is created only after selecting Play. The guide and checklist are available beforehand; optional videos may contain spoilers.
